If you move cargo in or out of Pakistan, container transport from Karachi to Lahore is probably the route you deal with most. It connects the country's only deep-water container gateway to its largest inland consumption and manufacturing market, and it carries a bigger share of container traffic than any other inland lane in the country. At PK Transporters, this is our highest-volume route by a wide margin, which also means it's the one we get asked the most honest questions about: What will it actually cost? How long does it really take? Should I book a 20ft or a 40ft unit?

## The Route: How a Container Actually Moves from Karachi to Lahore

A container leaving Karachi Port or Port Qasim for Lahore has two realistic corridor choices, and most carriers — including us — default to the faster one unless there's a reason not to.

**Option 1: M-9 → N-5 National Highway.** The container moves out of the port area, joins the **M-9 Motorway** to Hyderabad, then continues north on the **N-5 National Highway** (the old GT Road corridor) through Sukkur, Multan, and on to Lahore. This is the traditional route and still carries a large share of heavy/oversized and non-containerized freight because sections of the N-5 have fewer restrictions than the motorway network.

**Option 2: M-9 → M-5 → M-2 Motorway corridor.** From Hyderabad, traffic can shift onto the **M-5 Motorway** (Sukkur–Multan) and then the **M-2 Motorway** (Lahore–Islamabad, run in reverse toward Lahore) for a fully motorway-grade run into the city. This corridor generally has better surface quality, fewer at-grade crossings, and more predictable travel speeds, which is why most container trailers running this lane today prefer it whenever axle-weight and toll conditions allow.

In practice, the exact combination a driver takes on any given day depends on weather, ongoing roadworks, and toll-plaza congestion — but the corridor is consistently the same north–south backbone: Karachi, Hyderabad, Sukkur/Multan, Lahore. If you want the full story of how a container moves from port gate to final delivery on any Pakistani route, our guide on [how container transport works in Pakistan](/blog/how-container-transport-works-in-pakistan) walks through the process end to end.

The road distance between Karachi and Lahore by this corridor is commonly cited in the range of roughly **1,150–1,250 km**, depending on the exact combination of motorway and national highway segments used and the specific pickup and delivery points within each city. We deliberately give a range rather than a single figure here — unlike a straight-line distance, the actual kilometers driven shift with route choice, and we'd rather be accurate than falsely precise.

## Realistic Transit Time: What to Actually Plan For

The figure most commonly quoted across the Pakistani logistics industry for a laden container running Karachi to Lahore is **2 to 3 days**, door to door. That range accounts for:

- Loading and gate-out formalities at the port terminal
- The driving time itself, which for a laden prime mover on this corridor typically runs well over a full day given legal driving-hour limits, rest breaks, and average laden speeds
- Toll plaza and checkpoint stops along the motorway and highway network
- Any delay at weigh stations if the load needs to be verified against axle-weight limits
- Final-mile delivery and offloading once the container reaches the Lahore side

Treat 2–3 days as the industry-typical planning window rather than a guarantee. Monsoon-season flooding on the N-5 corridor, fog on the motorway network (a recurring winter issue in central Punjab), Eid and public-holiday traffic surges, and port congestion at the origin can all push a shipment toward the longer end of that range or beyond it. If your business runs on tight production schedules — a textile mill waiting on raw material, for example — build a buffer day into your planning rather than treating the shorter end of the range as the default.

For time-critical shipments, ask your carrier for a confirmed pickup slot and a driver-tracked ETA rather than a generic quote. This is one of the reasons a dedicated [container haulage](/services/container-haulage) provider with its own trailer fleet on this corridor tends to be more reliable than a broker reselling capacity.

## What Actually Drives the Rate

We're not going to hand you a fixed rupee figure here, because a single published number would be stale within weeks — fuel prices in Pakistan are reviewed and adjusted frequently, and container rates track them closely. What we can do is show you the actual cost factors that go into a quote, so you can read any rate you're given (from us or anyone else) and understand what's behind it.

| Cost Factor | What It Reflects |
|---|---|
| **Base linehaul rate** | Distance-based cost of running the prime mover and trailer the ~1,150–1,250 km corridor, including the driver's return leg |
| **Fuel surcharge** | Tracks the current High-Speed Diesel (HSD) price, which is reviewed regularly by the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA) and is the single largest variable input in any long-haul quote |
| **Container size & weight** | 20ft vs 40ft, and whether the cargo approaches the legal gross vehicle weight ceiling — heavier loads cost more to move and may require weight-compliant axle configurations |
| **Terminal & documentation charges** | Port gate-out fees, terminal handling, and paperwork processing at origin |
| **Destination access** | Whether the delivery point is an easy-access industrial zone (e.g., Sundar or Quaid-e-Azam Industrial Estate) or a congested inner-city location requiring a smaller vehicle for last-mile transfer |
| **Seasonal demand** | Rates can firm up during peak textile export seasons or ahead of major holidays when trailer capacity tightens |

Diesel is worth underlining on its own. Road freight in Pakistan runs almost entirely on High-Speed Diesel, and HSD prices have moved often through 2026 — both up and down — as OGRA's pricing mechanism responds to international product prices and exchange-rate movements. We've covered exactly how this works in a previous post on how [petrol and diesel price changes flow through to container transport rates](/blog/petrol-diesel-price-drop-container-transport-rates-pakistan); the short version is that because fuel is such a large share of the linehaul cost on a long corridor like Karachi–Lahore, any credible quote you receive should move in the same direction as the current diesel price, not lag behind it by months.

*The cost factors above describe how a rate is built, not a fixed price. Actual quotes vary by container weight, cargo type, current fuel prices, and delivery location — [request a quote](/get-quote) for a firm, itemised number on your specific shipment.*

## 20ft vs 40ft Container: Which One for This Route?

Karachi–Lahore is a high-enough-volume corridor that both container sizes move constantly, so the choice usually comes down to your cargo, not the route.

**Choose a 20ft container when:**
- Your cargo is dense and heavy relative to its volume (machinery parts, steel, chemicals in drums) — a 20ft unit reaches its weight limit before it fills up on volume
- You're shipping a partial load and don't want to pay for unused 40ft space
- You need more scheduling flexibility, since 20ft units are generally easier to slot into a departure

**Choose a 40ft container when:**
- Your cargo is bulky but relatively light (finished textiles, garments, FMCG cartons, packaging materials) — you'll fill the volume well before hitting the weight ceiling
- You're consolidating a full production run or a large FMCG/pharma distribution shipment in a single move
- You want a lower cost-per-CBM, since the 40ft rate is not double the 20ft rate despite carrying roughly double the volume

If you're still unsure which unit fits your cargo, our dedicated comparison on [the difference between 20ft and 40ft containers](/blog/difference-between-20ft-and-40ft-containers) goes through the numbers in more detail. Either way, book through a carrier that runs both — see our [20ft container transport](/services/20ft-container-transport) and [40ft container transport](/services/40ft-container-transport) services — rather than one that only operates a single trailer type, since that limits your flexibility if your cargo mix changes mid-shipment.

## Documentation You'll Need for an Inland Container Move

Moving a container inland from Karachi to Lahore isn't a customs event in itself (that happens at the port), but it still requires a clean paper trail. At minimum, expect to have ready:

- **Delivery order / gate-out pass** from the shipping line or terminal, authorizing the container's release
- **Bill of lading or its equivalent** reference number, tying the container to the original shipment
- **Container interchange receipt (EIR)**, documenting the container's condition at gate-out
- **Weight/axle declaration**, since Pakistani highways enforce gross vehicle weight limits and mismatched declared vs. actual weight can hold up a shipment at a weigh station
- **Consignee and delivery address details**, including any site-specific access requirements (industrial estate permits, loading dock restrictions, night-delivery rules)
- **Insurance documentation** for the cargo, if arranged separately from the carrier

If your shipment is arriving from overseas and this is your first time managing the port-to-door leg, it's worth reading through our guide on [customs clearance](/services/customs-clearance) and [Karachi Port logistics](/blog/karachi-port-logistics-guide) before the container even lands, so the inland leg isn't held up waiting on paperwork that could have been prepared in advance.

## What Makes This Corridor Different on the Ground

Numbers and route maps only tell part of the story. Anyone who has actually run trailers on this corridor for years will tell you that a lot of what makes a shipment go smoothly has nothing to do with the highway itself.

Most repeat shippers on this route don't re-tender every load — they stick with a transporter they trust, often built over years of on-time deliveries and honest handling of the odd delay or damage claim. That trust matters more here than a written contract, because so much of the day-to-day coordination — a changed pickup time, a gate pass that's ready early, a driver stuck at a weighbridge — gets sorted over a phone call or a WhatsApp message, not email. If you're calling a new transporter and they're slow to pick up WhatsApp, that's usually a signal about how the rest of the shipment will go.

A driver who has run Karachi–Lahore for years also carries knowledge you won't find in any manual: which rest stops are safe and reliable overnight, how to time the run through Sukkur or Multan to avoid getting boxed in by city traffic, and how the route quirks shift once monsoon rains hit low-lying stretches of the N-5. Weighbridge and checkpost stops are also handled differently by an experienced crew — knowing what documentation to have ready and how to move through a checkpost without unnecessary delay is a learned skill, not something a rate sheet captures.

Finally, most importers don't deal with WeBOC and terminal formalities directly — they rely on a clearing agent, and the transporter's actual working relationship with staff at the Karachi Port terminal gates (KICT, PICT, or Port Qasim) often determines whether a container leaves the yard on schedule or sits waiting. A transporter who owns trailers but has no real standing at the gate is not the same as one whose trucks are recognized and waved through.

## FAQ: Container Transport from Karachi to Lahore

**Q: How far is it from Karachi to Lahore by road for a container shipment?**
The commonly cited road distance is roughly 1,150–1,250 km, depending on the specific combination of the M-9, M-5/M-2 motorways, or N-5 National Highway used, and the exact pickup and delivery points at each end.

**Q: How long does a container take to travel from Karachi to Lahore?**
Industry-typical transit time is 2 to 3 days door to door, accounting for port gate-out, driving-hour limits, toll and checkpoint stops, and final-mile delivery. Weather, holidays, and congestion can extend this.

**Q: Should I book a 20ft or 40ft container for this route?**
It depends on your cargo. Dense, heavy cargo that hits weight limits before filling the space suits a 20ft unit; bulky but lighter cargo that fills volume before reaching the weight limit suits a 40ft unit, generally at a lower cost per CBM. See our [20ft vs 40ft comparison](/blog/difference-between-20ft-and-40ft-containers) for detail.

**Q: What determines the freight rate on the Karachi–Lahore route?**
The main factors are the base linehaul distance, the current diesel (HSD) price and fuel surcharge, container size and cargo weight, terminal and documentation charges, and how accessible the final delivery point is. Rates move with diesel prices, which are reviewed regularly by OGRA.

**Q: Which route do container trailers usually take — the motorway or the National Highway?**
Most modern container trailers prefer the M-9 to M-5/M-2 motorway corridor where possible, since it generally offers better surface quality and more predictable travel times than the N-5 National Highway, though the N-5 still carries significant freight traffic.

**Q: What documents do I need ready for an inland move from Karachi to Lahore?**
At minimum: the delivery order/gate-out pass, bill of lading reference, container interchange receipt, a weight declaration, and full consignee delivery details. If the shipment is arriving from overseas, customs clearance at the port needs to be finalized before the inland leg can start.
